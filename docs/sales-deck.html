02 What it costs to get wrong

The mistakes are small. The consequences arrive months later.

A wrong tax split

Charging IGST where CGST + SGST was due — or the reverse — is one wrong state code. It surfaces when GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B stop reconciling, by which point every invoice to that customer carries the same error.

A duplicated invoice number

A tax invoice series has to be consecutive within the financial year. A gap is a question with an answer. Two invoices carrying the same number is a question without one, and it cannot be undone.

A GSTIN that belongs to nobody

A transposed pair of characters passes a format check. The cost lands on your customer, whose input tax credit is refused — and then on you, when they ask why.

Credit you never claimed

Purchase bills that were never matched against GSTR-2B are input tax credit sitting unclaimed, and a supplier who did not file is credit at risk that nobody told you about.

05 What it does not do yet

The list above is short by choice.

E-invoicing (IRN) and e-way bills are not live.

Everything around them is built and tested — eligibility, validation, the payload, the validity window — but the connection to the government portal is not written, so nothing can actually be filed. They go on the list the day that adapter ships and not a day before.

We are telling you this on the pricing page rather than after you have paid, because the alternative is you finding out during your first month-end. If e-invoicing is mandatory for your turnover today, this is not yet the product for you.
